<div class="sec "><p class="kicker">The format</p><h2>Two quantities, one relationship</h2><ul class="points"><li><b>A</b>&nbsp; Quantity A is greater</li><li><b>B</b>&nbsp; Quantity B is greater</li><li><b>C</b>&nbsp; The two quantities are equal</li><li><b>D</b>&nbsp; The relationship cannot be determined from the information given</li></ul></div>

<!--
Point out: if both quantities are plain numbers with no variables, D is never the answer. A fixed number has one value.
-->

---
layout: none
---

<div class="sec "><p class="kicker">Three strategies</p><h2>Choose your tool before you do any math</h2><div class="cards3"><div class="card" v-click><p class="num">1</p><h3>Simplify both sides</h3><p class="txt2">Do the same legal operation to both quantities, or rewrite them in matching forms (same denominator, same base, benchmarks) until the comparison is obvious.</p></div><div class="card" v-click><p class="num">2</p><h3>Pick numbers</h3><p class="txt2">When variables are involved, test values that follow the rules, including fractions, negatives and zero where allowed. Two different results means D.</p></div><div class="card" v-click><p class="num">3</p><h3>Assume equal, then adjust</h3><p class="txt2">If one side is a number and the other is unknown, pretend they’re equal and see whether that breaks a rule. The direction it breaks tells you which side is bigger.</p></div></div></div>

<!--
Spend the most time on strategy 3. It’s the least intuitive and the most underused.
-->
